Sofia E Mattson

College of Human Ecology, Cornell University

I've earned an M.S. in Human-Environment Relations at Cornell, in the Department of Human Centered Design, after a B.S. in Psychology. Before Cornell, I spent four years in affective neuroscience labs studying how threat impacts working memory — from which I co-authored two published articles — and two years on the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development study running psychiatric interviews, neuropsychological testing, and multimodal MRI. I also am a classically trained harpist of twenty years and worked as a certified early childhood music teacher for cognitive development before attending graduate school.

That background is why I value evidence-based decision making, critical approaches to data and design, and the range and depth of human experience. It also shapes what I choose to work on: hospitals, workplaces, classrooms, and the virtual complements of these spaces to improve existing infrastructure, workflows, and lifestyles.

01

The Challenge

As more healthcare organizations adopt AI architecture into existing EHR systems, one of the leading causes of post-launch failure is undetected model drift.

Our client, KP, already had the vision for community integration through technology from their Project RAD’s Health Hub, but nothing connected clinical data, lifestyle, local resources and population trends. Physicians and members were filling that gap with unregulated tools like ChatGPT and Doximity.

As we presented to Kasier's Architectural and Tech Innovation team, we wanted AI to make facility investment measurable, and in turn, the facility design makes the AI useful via predictive modeling given member and physician context.


02

Key Decisions

i. We chose an on-premise, data-centric model over a cloud, model-centric one

ii. We made governance a 90-second micro-task between encounters, not a parallel administrative job

iii. We placed the room beside exam and conference rooms, away from member corridors, and keycard-locked it

03

System design

04

Outcome

A three-year phased rollout with calculated risks for missed milestones. Doctors' new role of governing AI output warrants physical infrastructure to support the human side of this necessary task. The governance space is designed to complement the on-premise, data-centric model; providing evidence-based design suggestions for maximum physician comfort and convenience reinforces strong data. 

8–10 min

Clinician time reclaimed per visit, from the pre-visit AI brief plus a 90-second governance check.

$8M

Capital at risk if model drift goes undetected — the cost the space exists to prevent. Amenity ROI is made visible.

90 sec

Timed review per AI output: amber at 75 seconds, then approve, override, escalate, or defer.

01

The Challenge

Social isolation carries a 29% increase in mortality risk, and 75% of US adults over 50 want to age in place while only half believe their community is equipped for it. Physical third spaces and digital platforms both exist; nothing connects them. Plus, each activity and space is filtered for various accessibility needs.

We analyzed competitors to understand the gap — Nextdoor broadcasts, Wyzr borrows dating-app swipe logic, and Wheelmap catalogues venues with no social layer. Then we let the focus groups redirect us four separate times. The first redirect: starting out with an intergenerational framing diluted our solution, so we recentered on older adults to emphasize aging-in-place.

This project is ongoing. We are employing the Delphi method to build a consensus for certified age-friendly checklist items among individuals aged 60+ across the US.

02

Key Decisions

i. We made it physical-first: digital matching exists to get people into a room

ii. We activated existing venues instead of planning new assisted living

iii. We moved from one-on-one pairs to small groups

03

Outcome

An average usability score of 73.5 across testers, range 60–82.5. Concerns reported include privacy and reaching introverted members.

The Challenge

People bring their most personal questions to AI systems that answer with the fluency of an oracle and none of the accountability of a clinician. The combination of human vulnerability to self-labeling and computational (in)capabilities of instant data processing potentially create escalating dependencies rather than providing insights or genuine healing.

02

Key Decisions

i. We gave AI a familiar body: a magic-8 ball

ii. When intelligence is embodied, does it interpret how you touch it?

03

Outcome

A working object and a pictorial documenting the process. The outcome is the subversive argument the artifact makes when someone picks it up and feels 'read'. Giving AI a body then affords behavior which may be expected from a body condemned to eternal servitude and physical handling.

01

The Challenge

Data centers replace habitat, and the most documented harm to their neighbors is noise. Residents in Arizona, Arkansas, and Texas report a 24/7 hum that earplugs don't remediate, and complaints go unresolved.

02

Key Decisions

i. I changed one variable: the same recording, with and without the drone

ii. I didn't tell anyone what they were hearing, or where

03

Outcome

Listeners heard the waterfall as a highway or a Chicago L train and still called the scene nature. The intervention's legibility is not self-evident; people were transported to places in their own memory that featured both nature and built infrastructure.

Birdsong naturalized anything.

Even the version with the drone underneath was still called "nature" by listeners who heard the waterfall as a highway.

One heard the drone as meditative.

The same signal read as intrusion to some and as calm to another — legibility of the intervention is not self-evident.

What one participant said, unprompted

"People born without any experience of real nature will have nothing to compare a recording to, and may eventually find server hum soothing. That is shifting baseline syndrome, arrived at independently — and it locates the value of the whole intervention. The soundscape does not work because of what it contains. It works in the gap between the recording and the memory it activates. As fewer people hold that referent, the critique loses its force."

Scope note: five participants, convenience sample, no demographics collected. Treated as formative and illustrative, consistent with research through design.

01

The Challenge

The clinic needed a swing space that served the broader community and offered more spatial and resource flexibility for partnering organizations working at the current SACP. Overall, the workflow for faculty, students, and staff did not align with the intended layout of the facility.

02

Key Decisions

i. Programming approaches: behavior circuit & user profile

ii. We measured animal comfort with a validated feline stress indicator

iii. We programmed for mixed use and flexible teams while minimizing interspecies contact

03

Outcome

Five prioritized issues translated into program requirements: circulation congestion, species separation and storage, dedicated workspace, environmental control, and amenities. Surgery was reprogrammed for flexible teams.

Reception set the tone. Of every room, reception showed the most validated stress indicators in cats, where waiting is less tolerable than the treatment for both the animal client and its caregiver.

Storage was the biggest staff stressor. Inefficient storage generated more workflow breakdowns than any other issue, by both qualitative and quantitative count.

Serving wider community is complex. Spontaneous surgeries and strays carrying illness demand spatial flexibility, so we designed for mixed use and teamwork while minimizing interspecies contact.

01

The Challenge

Clinical VR research treats virtual reality nature landscapes as interchangeable for therapeutic stress reduction. That means that any sufficiently green scene is assumed to restore equally, whatever the viewer's history with what's depicted. If familiarity matters, a one-size-fits-all scene library may be restorative for some patients, but not all.

02

Key Decisions

i. I matched the two environments on waterfall geometry, duration, and affordances

ii. I paired each exposure with a Stroop stressor

iii. I restricted the primary analysis to first exposures

03

Outcome

Anxiety fell further after the congruent environment (−8.40 vs −3.62 STAI-S, d = 0.70) — the predicted direction at a moderate-to-large effect, non-significant in an underpowered sample.


Thematic analysis of 17 semi-structured interviews found asymmetric place recognition (13 versus 8 participants) alongside a near-universal critique of ecological sparseness. Regional congruence was perceived as intended but could not be isolated; an adequately powered replication requires approximately 68 participants.

01

The Challenge

Advice on interior planting is contested — more greenery is usually better, until it isn't. Neuroticism and openness are known to influence perceived restoration ratings, so the same room may not restore everyone equally.

02

Key Decisions

i. I used VR to hold the interior constant and vary only vegetation density

ii. I measured personality alongside restoration

03

Outcome

Neither environment was significantly more restorative. Coherence ran higher in low density (3.78 vs 3.06, closest to significance at p = .10) while fascination ran higher in high density (3.56 vs 2.89) — order and interest pulling against each other. Openness tracked negatively with coherence (r = −.75).

01

The Challenge

Classrooms with windows viewing nature are known to help cognitive performance. Evidence is lacking for where windows are placed and students are faced. In other words, can students better concentrate when they face towards or away from the windows?

02

Key Decisions

i. I used the same classroom and changed which way participants faced

ii. I crossed orientation with age

03

Outcome

Against prediction, facing the window was slower on average (88.26 s vs 80.39 s) with more errors. Age moderated it: under-26s were slightly faster facing the window (72.27 s vs 74.79 s), while over-26s were markedly slower (104.25 s vs 85.98 s). At N = 8 this is a signal to replicate, not a result to act on.
